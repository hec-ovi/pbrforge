# Used urban hard surfaces

Ten original AI image generations provide continuous concrete, fine aggregate asphalt, faded red coating, weathered concrete, teal paint, satin metal, cream lacquer, broad wiped residue, fine fingerprints, and a transparent six-type decal sheet. Prompts are in `prompts/`; unmodified outputs in `raw/`. No reference screenshots are included. Existing generated stone, walnut and terracotta sources from `sources/interior-kinds/` supply those substrates.

Run `npm run build`, then `node scripts/surface-remaster.mjs masks`, `streets`, `exteriors`, and `interiors` sequentially. The recipe flattens broad illumination drift, cross-fades offset copies at both repeat edges, and prepares coordinated maps under `out/surface-remaster/`. Every set enters the catalog through `pbrforge create --native` and is resolved immediately. The prepared import preserves independent roughness instead of estimating it from relief. `node scripts/sync-remaster-catalog.mjs` preserves existing aliases and exact exterior companions; `node scripts/bind-surface-remaster.mjs` publishes street and shared-detail bindings. Run `npm run compress -- --workers 2 --keys sources/surface-remaster/keys.json`, then the catalog synchronization once more to publish compressed references on shared companions.

`catalog/` records every key, variant, physical repeat, resolution, roughness statistics and albedo wrap check. Normal/height contain shallow physical relief; wiped gloss is independent. Grayscale PNG data channels are linear, albedo is sRGB, normals +Y. Packed response is R=1, G=roughness, B=metallic. PNGs are masters; KTX2 siblings carry mipmaps and published hashes in the bindings.

Street slabs repeat at 2 m with 6 mm joints, small paired anchor recesses and rougher rubbed borders. Painted slabs expose grey primer at worn edges. Curbs repeat at 2 × 0.25 m. Hex embossing is shallow, with fine dark borders, and two colour variants. Asphalt has clean, tyre-polished and patched variants. Road paint, grates and slotted drains have their own complete sets. Existing native street surface IDs bind to the new sources, including the formerly near-black district sidewalk panels.

Poor concrete retains vertical mineral and rust traces, pores and patchy colour. Teal paint carries tiny primer chips and service wear. Poor and mid concrete, graphite coating, painted metal and service alloy are remastered; exact companions remain identical. Interior finishes include octagonal ivory tiles with dark square inserts, E1 cream lacquer and teal stone, steel panels and worktop, walnut, gold trim, C4 terracotta and teal paint, and capsule ivory enamel.

Shared detail keys use `cyberpunk/surface-detail/`: `broad-smudge` (2 m), `fine-fingerprint` (0.25 m), `edge-scuff` and `edge-chips` (1 m), `dirt-dust` and `dirt-streaks` (2 m). All are 2048², tileable, grayscale, sampled from `roughness.r`. These are coverage masks, not literal physical roughness. `bindings/surface-detail.json` declares the role, channel and scale. The generated scalar sources are resampled to 2048²; their original generation size is retained in `raw/`.

The `decal-atlas` entry has `engine-grid` first: a transparent 2048² 4×4 atlas compatible with the engine detail layer. Row 0 contains oil and tyre residues; row 1 cracks; row 2 water marks; row 3 gum and paint chips. Each cell has transparent gutters. The `six-marks` variant keeps the 3×2 layout (tyres, oil, paint chips / cracks, gum, water). Both carry independent opacity and roughness, plus basecolor alpha. UV rectangles and individual categories are in the shared binding.

Verification: `node scripts/verify-surface-remaster.mjs` checks every remastered variant, PNG and compressed binding, packed channels, tiled seams, non-flat roughness, KTX2 dimensions, codecs, transfer functions and complete mip chains. `node scripts/package-surface-remaster.mjs` creates the versioned overlay archive under `out/` and its committed SHA-256 inventory under `releases/`. Build outputs remain untracked, following the existing compression convention.
