# Hex and terracotta condition sources

Eight grey hex, eight orange hex and eight terracotta variants share their respective physical construction frames. Each family has clean, stained, cracked and patched histories with two arrangements per condition. Grey/orange pigmentation shares a mineral source and hex construction; these are pigment variants, not separate scans. The clay family uses its own generated photographic source. Preserved inputs and prompt hashes are in [source-inventory.json](source-inventory.json); all sources are synthetic photographic imagery.

Hex maps are 1024 × 1182 at 1.2 × 1.385640646 m, with 100 mm circumradius, shallow inset rings and small centre recesses. Native mineral/wipe inputs are cropped proportionally from 1254² before reduction; there is no source upscaling. Terracotta maps are 1024² at 1.2² m, with 200 × 150 mm staggered clay modules and narrow joints. Tile kiln tones remain stable across wear states; replacements alter selected whole modules. All maps are nonmetal, sRGB base colour, linear data, and +Y normals derived from the physical height field. Height zero is 0.5 with 0.008 m per unit. Broad oil/dust changes response, not deep displacement.

[Catalog inventory](catalog.json) names every complete PBR map set. [Portable requests](portable-publish-requests.json) use paths relative to the generated authoring stage. `node scripts/author-street-tile-families.mjs` reconstructs sources, prepared maps and the isolated native database in `out/tile-families-authoring`. `node scripts/check-authored-street-tile-families.mjs` verifies that staged output. The preserved publisher requires an explicit `--themes` target, resolves keys before append, and relies on native create's complete packed maps; it does not repack unrelated target entries.

The street companion binds the hex and clay families at their catalog metre periods. `scripts/bind-metric-tile-surfaces.mjs` corrects native `terracotta` from orange hex to actual clay and binds `basalt` to grey hex. Both roles use the existing world-XZ sampler at their physical repeat, as the producer emits horizontal paving polygons. Their block palette selection, geometry and authored UV attributes remain intact; this is a material route/calibration change and introduces no engine sampler code. Variant selection still requires the companion consumer. Curbs and lane lines are untouched.

Technical checks pass for all 168 PBR maps: shared family boundaries, wrap edges, physical scale, packed channels and normal direction/length. Render and reference acceptance remain pending. Clean variants differ mainly in gloss; fine fractures may fade at distance; the fixed kiln-tone layout can still repeat. Eight histories alone do not erase every repeated feature.
